What you'll need

  • An external USB CD/DVD drive — most current Macs don't have a built-in one
  • Ripping software (a few good options below)
  • A bit of patience for a large collection — figure a few minutes per disc

Choosing a format

This is the one decision that's hard to undo later, since re-ripping means finding the disc again.

Lossless (ALAC or FLAC)

An exact copy of the CD audio, just compressed without losing any data. Takes more space (roughly 300–400MB per album) but means you'll never need to rip the disc again for a better copy. ALAC plays natively in Apple's ecosystem; FLAC is the more universal choice if you use other software too.

AAC 256kbps

Indistinguishable from the original for most people and most listening situations, at a fraction of the file size. The reasonable choice if storage space matters more than having a bit-perfect archival copy.

Get the tags right

A rip without correct metadata is just an unorganized pile of audio files. Whichever tool you use, confirm before you rip that it found the right album — album art, track titles, artist, and release year all come from an online lookup (usually Gracenote or MusicBrainz), and it can occasionally match the wrong pressing or a compilation instead of the studio album. A minute spent checking now saves a library full of mislabeled tracks later.
