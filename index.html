Your phone shows your library. It doesn't keep it.

Syncing and iCloud's Download Originals put a copy of your library on every device, so a library bigger than your phone eventually fills it. Raza Photos and Raza Songs stream from your Mac as you browse or play. The only storage that matters is your Mac's, or an external drive you plug into it, and your phone's space stays yours. Open the app, and your library is there.

Your Mac already has your photos and music. Raza Photos and Raza Songs announce those folders on your home Wi-Fi, so your iPhone and iPad find the library on their own. There is no address to type, no sign-in and nothing to configure on the phone. Your files never move, and nothing is copied to the phone. Getting started →

What you won't be asked to do.

The usual ways to see a library on your phone ask for one of these. Raza Photos and Raza Songs ask for none of them.

Run a server program

The Mac app is the whole system. There is nothing separate to install, update or keep running, and no containers, databases or config files.

Create an account

No sign-up for you and no logins for the family. Access is a read-only toggle per device, not a user list to maintain.

Pay to unlock your own library

No feature sits behind a plan. Free today, and if that ever changes it will be a one-time purchase, never a subscription.

Hand over your organizing work

Dates, places, people and albums are written into your own files in open standards. If Raza stopped tomorrow, your library would still be exactly where it is. How saving works →

Two apps, each built for what it holds.

Both are native Swift apps, with HDR, Live Photos, on-device face recognition, gapless audio, CarPlay and AirPlay. Edit metadata from your phone, send photos from your iPhone to your Mac, put a memory on your Home Screen, merge several Macs into one library, or reach it away from home through optional, encrypted, invite-only Tailscale. These are the conveniences you'd expect from a cloud service, without the cloud, the account or the monthly bill.

Privacy is architectural, not a promise: your library only ever goes to your own devices, never to our servers or anyone else's, and the apps have no telemetry. Nothing is exposed to the internet by default, and you can check all of it yourself. How to verify it →

One developer, who answers the email.

Raza Software is one developer in the EU, and there is no company behind it today. Support emails go to that person, and you'll hear back from a human, not a queue. If the apps ever stop, your library will be exactly where it is, readable by any app that opens those files. Who we are →

Built for households, not sysadmins.

Raza Photos and Raza Songs are for the family that wants its photos and music on every device in the house, and doesn't want to make that a hobby. They're built for Mac, iPhone and iPad. They aren't open source, and they don't run on Linux or Windows.

If you enjoy running your own stack, containers and config files included, there are excellent open-source projects made for exactly that, and they're a better fit. If you'd rather just see the photos from your Mac on your phone at the dinner table, this is for you.
