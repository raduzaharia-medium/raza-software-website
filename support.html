Every Mac running the app on the same network advertises its library independently. On iPhone and iPad, all available libraries appear merged into a single unified view. Apple requires apps to ask your permission before they can listen for Bonjour announcements or connect to devices on your local network. This is a privacy protection — it prevents apps from silently scanning your network in the background. When the app asks for local network access, it is asking for exactly the permission it needs to find your Mac, and nothing more. Modern routers broadcast on both 2.4GHz and 5GHz simultaneously but keep them on the same network segment. Devices on different bands can still find each other via Bonjour — band is a radio frequency choice, not a network boundary. The exception is if your router has "band steering" or "AP isolation" enabled, which some routers do incorrectly — if discovery fails, checking those settings is a good first step. Yes. If your Mac is connected via Ethernet and your iPhone is on Wi-Fi, Bonjour still works — they are on the same local network regardless of how they connect to the router. A wired Mac will actually serve your library faster since it has a more stable, higher-bandwidth connection to the router. Guest networks are a separate network segment from your main network. Bonjour announcements from your Mac do not cross that boundary — they stay within the main network. A device on the guest network simply never hears the announcement, so it never knows the library exists. This is not a setting — it is a structural property of how your router separates the networks.
