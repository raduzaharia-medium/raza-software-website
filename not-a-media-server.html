What a media server is

A media server is a program you install on a computer or a NAS. It builds its own catalog of your movies, shows, music and photos, and streams them to apps on your TVs, phones and tablets. Plenty of people love them, and for a large video collection on many screens they are the right tool.

What they usually ask of you

You install the server and keep it updated. You create user accounts, and in many products an online account too. You install an app on each device, and in some products features such as remote access or downloads sit behind a paid plan. Your catalog, and the organizing work you do in it, usually lives in the server's own database. Video comes first, and photos and music are sometimes handled by separate or secondary apps.

That's a lot to take on just to see your own photos and play your own music.

What Raza asks of you instead

Install Raza Photos or Raza Songs on your Mac, pick the folders where your photos or music already live, and switch on library sharing. Open the app on your iPhone or iPad and the library is there. There is no separate server program, no account, no plan, and no catalog to rebuild: your files stay where they are, and the changes you make from your phone are written back into them in open formats. When a media server is the better choice

If your collection is mostly movies and shows, if you want live TV or recording, if you want to watch on a TV, a game console or an Android phone, if each family member needs their own profile and parental controls, or if you want the library to live on a NAS instead of a Mac, a media server does what Raza doesn't. Raza Photos and Raza Songs run on Apple devices only, and the Mac has to be on.

When Raza is

Your photos and music already sit in folders on a Mac. You want them on every iPhone and iPad in the house without running a server, creating accounts or paying a monthly fee. And you want the work you put into organizing them to live in your own files, so that it outlasts any app, including this one.
