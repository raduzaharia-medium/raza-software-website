1. Get Raza Photos ready on your Mac

Install Raza Photos on your Mac and turn on library sharing so your iPhone can find it. Then open the Transfer settings on the Mac, switch on Allow incoming transfers (it's off until you do), and pick the folder where your photos should land. Your originals keep their filenames, and photos are sorted into year and month subfolders automatically.

2. Send your whole library from the iPhone

Install Raza Photos on your iPhone and open the Transfer sheet. Your Mac appears in the list automatically; pick it. Raza Photos reads your photo library on the phone, including the photos that live in iCloud, and sends your full history, not just new shots, to your Mac over your home Wi-Fi. No cable, no export, no manual download.

  • 1Open Raza Photos on the iPhone and open the Transfer sheet
  • 2Pick your Mac as the destination
  • 3Keep the app open while it catches up. iOS only lets transfers run while the app is in the foreground
  • 4Leave "delete originals from this device" off for now. You can decide after step 3

Every Mac on your network with incoming transfers enabled shows up automatically. From then on, just browsing is enough — photos send quietly in the background.

When nothing is left to send, your photos are on the Mac. Browse them in Raza Photos on any device in the house to see that everything arrived.

3. Back up the Mac before you turn iCloud off

This is the honest tradeoff of leaving the cloud: once iCloud Photos is off, your Mac may hold the only full copy of your library. Back it up first. Time Machine to an external drive takes minutes to set up, and a second drive kept somewhere else covers you against fire and theft. A simple backup plan →

4. Turn off iCloud Photos

With your library on the Mac and backed up, turn off iCloud Photos on every device — Mac, iPhone, and iPad. On each device, this lives under Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Photos. Your photos stay exactly where they already are on each device; only the syncing between them stops. To stop paying for storage as well, see how to cancel iCloud storage safely.

5. Browse from your iPhone and iPad — no iCloud required

With iCloud Photos off, your iPhone's camera roll and your Mac's library are two separate things again. Raza Photos bridges that gap over your home Wi-Fi: open the app on any iPhone, iPad, or other Mac in the house, and it finds your library automatically using Bonjour — no IP address, no account, no setup.

6. Keep new iPhone photos flowing in, without iCloud

The one thing iCloud Photos used to handle automatically was getting new photos off your iPhone. The same Transfer feature replaces it: new photos and videos send to your Mac over Wi-Fi as you browse. When you're happy that everything arrives safely, you can switch on the option to clear originals from the phone once they're transferred. How Transfer works →
