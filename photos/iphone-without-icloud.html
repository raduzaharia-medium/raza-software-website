Why this is possible without iCloud

iCloud Photos solves a problem you don't actually have if your iPhone and your Mac are usually on the same Wi-Fi network: getting a photo from one device to another. Instead of uploading every photo to a server and downloading it back down, your iPhone can just ask your Mac directly — over the network you're already on, in your own house.

How to set it up

  • 1Install Raza Photos on your Mac and point it at the folder your photos are already in
  • 2It indexes the metadata — dates, locations, people, albums — without moving or copying a single file
  • 3Start the server from the app, so it can be found on your home network
  • 4Install Raza Photos on your iPhone or iPad and open it
  • 5Your Mac's library shows up automatically, discovered over Bonjour — no IP address, no login, no setup

What you get

  • Browse by date, location, people, albums, and events — from your sidebar
  • Map view, full-screen viewer, HDR and Live Photos rendered natively
  • Works entirely over your home Wi-Fi — no internet connection needed
  • Multiple Macs on the network merge automatically into one library
  • Nothing is ever uploaded anywhere, at any point

One thing to know

This only works while your iPhone is on the same network as your Mac — or connected to it directly, without an intervening cloud. Away from home, there's nothing to connect to, which is a deliberate tradeoff for having no server outside your house at all. If your Mac is usually asleep, turning on Wake for network access in Energy settings keeps it reachable.
