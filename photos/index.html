Metadata

Your data stays portable.

Raza Photos reads from seven metadata standards and writes back to open formats — so nothing is locked in, ever. Full metadata details →

What Raza Photos reads

  • Date taken with full timezone and format support
  • GPS coordinates, country and city
  • Titles from IPTC, XMP, or filename fallback
  • People names and face bounding boxes
  • Albums, events, and category hierarchies
  • Burst sequences from Apple MakerNote or XMP
  • File creation and modification dates
EXIF IPTC XMP GPS Apple MakerNote ACDSee XML Filesystem

What Raza Photos writes

  • Dates back to EXIF and TIFF standards
  • GPS coordinates to EXIF GPS dictionary
  • Titles to IPTC and XMP
  • Locations to IPTC, XMP, and ACDSee
  • People and face regions to XMP
  • Albums and events — compatible with Lightroom
  • Burst groupings via custom XMP namespace

Writes use CGImageDestinationCopyImageSource() — only metadata sections are touched. Your image pixels are never decoded or re-encoded.

Family controls

Let the kids browse.
Not rearrange.

Raza Photos gives you three levels of access control — no configuration wizard, no user accounts, no passwords to hand out.

🖥

Read-only server

Lock the Mac server entirely. Remote devices — every iPhone, iPad, or other Mac on the network — can browse the full library but cannot make any changes whatsoever. The library is yours to curate, from your Mac alone.

✏️

Block remote edits only

Allow browsing from any device on the network, but prevent metadata changes — no editing dates, locations, albums, or people tags from iOS. Browsing remains completely unrestricted.

📱

Read-only client

Set a specific iPhone or iPad to read-only mode on the device itself. That device becomes a pure viewer — no editing controls are shown, regardless of what the server allows.

FAQ

Common questions.

Do I need iCloud or an account to use Raza Photos?

No. There is no account, no sign-up and no iCloud. Your Mac serves the library over your home Wi-Fi, and your iPhone or iPad finds it automatically.

Does Raza Photos upload or move my photos?

No. It indexes the metadata and leaves every file exactly where it is. The app never makes an outbound network request and has no telemetry.

What happens when my Mac is off?

Your Mac is the server, so the library is available only while the Mac app is running. When the Mac is off there is nothing to connect to, and the library reappears as soon as it is back on your home network.

Can I browse my photos away from home?

Optionally. Remote access is an extension of your home network built on Tailscale. It is off by default, encrypted end to end, invite-only, and only reachable by devices already on your Tailscale network. Tailscale is separate software with its own setup.

Will editing on my iPhone change my original photos?

Only the metadata. Dates, locations, titles, people and albums are written back to the original files as XMP and EXIF. Image pixels are never decoded or re-encoded.

Can I stop other people in the house from changing my library?

Yes. You can make the Mac server read-only, block remote edits while still allowing browsing, or set a single iPhone or iPad to read-only. There are no accounts or passwords, just a toggle.

Which photo formats does Raza Photos support?

JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF, PSD and AVIF, plus the video formats Apple's frameworks support, with HDR gain maps and Live Photos.

Can I use more than one Mac?

Yes. Every Mac running the app is discovered automatically, and their libraries appear on your iPhone or iPad as one merged, deduplicated library.

Can I move my library to another app later?

Yes. Your files stay in your own folders, and organization such as albums, places and people is stored in open standards like EXIF, IPTC and XMP, which tools such as Lightroom read.

What do I need to run Raza Photos?

A Mac, iPhone or iPad running macOS 26 or iOS 26 or later.
