What syncing does

Apple's Finder sync copies content from your Mac onto your iPhone. For photos, you choose Photos or a folder, and Apple says the iPhone is then updated to match the albums on your computer. For music, you turn on music sync and choose your whole library or a selection. Syncing is for people who don't use iCloud Photos, and turning it off removes what was synced from the phone. If you subscribe to Apple Music, your library is managed through iCloud Music Library instead, and Finder's music sync is usually unavailable.

What streaming does

Raza Photos and Raza Songs leave your files on the Mac and serve them to your iPhone as you browse or play. Nothing is copied to the phone. Edits you make from the phone are written back to the original files on the Mac, so there is always exactly one copy that counts.

When syncing is the right tool

If you want your whole library on the phone for a long flight, or you spend time where your Mac isn't reachable, a copy on the phone wins. A small library that fits comfortably on the device is also a good case for it. Nothing about streaming beats having the file in your pocket.

When streaming is

A big library and a phone with limited storage. A family where several devices should see the same photos without each keeping its own copy. Two Macs that should look like one library. Or simply wanting to skip the cable and the sync step. Streaming spends no space on the phone and leaves nothing to reconcile.

Why we don't sync

It isn't that we can't. A synced copy takes space on every device and creates a second version of your library to keep in step with the first. We would rather keep one source of truth, your Mac, and stream from it. The tradeoff is honest and simple: the Mac has to be on. How library sharing works →
